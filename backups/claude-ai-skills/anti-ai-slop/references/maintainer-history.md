# anti-ai-slop Maintainer History

Version 1.12, owner Rock Lambros. Moved out of SKILL.md in 1.12 so it stops loading on every call. Read it before deleting or loosening any rule.

Maintainer note: this gate was added after a 2026-09-03 failure where the skill was named explicitly by the writer, never opened, and a pass was reported as complete. Do not remove it.

Second maintainer note: 1.2 came out of a 2026-09-20 review. Rock reported that all ten of his most recent corrections were tells that already had a rule, so the constraint was enforcement. 1.2 restores a bundled detector (`scripts/slop_lint.py`), removes two contradictions with coffee-peer-voice (this file prescribed fragments, and it required sentence case headings against Rock's Initial Cap Case standard), changes the repair order so a flagged word gets deleted or replaced with a fact before anyone looks for a synonym, and clears the replacement cells that offered a flagged word as the fix ("key," "highlights," "demonstrates," "significant," "turning point"). It also imports what a "Writing Rules" doc had that this catalog lacked. Three of that doc's terms stay off the catalog on purpose: "human oversight" is the title of EU AI Act Article 14, and "cross-functional" and "enablement" are working terms in Rock's proposals. The doc's instruction to vary vocabulary was rejected because it is the synonym cycling tell below.

Third maintainer note: 1.3 came out of a 2026-09-21 analysis of 2,971 emails Rock typed himself. The linter gained an `email` profile and three checks calibrated to his measured cadence (A-SMOOTH, A-LONG-PARAGRAPH, A-CONTRAST-BUDGET), because a draft that reads smoother than he writes is the cadence tell he keeps correcting. The data confirmed the catalog: the Tier 1 words are close to absent from his typing, and `align` is the one flagged word he uses often, which is why it stays advisory. Rock also decided that idiom he would say out loud is allowed while decorative metaphor and business cliche stay banned.

Fourth maintainer note: 1.6 came out of a 2026-10-09 Slack heads up that ended on "Wanted you to hear it from me before the intro goes out." and "Flag anything I should know first." Rock flagged both, and slop_lint 1.3.1 returned no HARD finding on the draft. slop_lint 1.4.0 adds H-CLOSER-MOTIVE for the first shape and A-CLOSER-GATE for the second, and reads only a message's last two sentences after any sign-off. That scope comes from Rock's rockcyber.com sent mail, searched the same day for seven of these phrases. The hits reviewed showed his own words only in openers and asks that carried a fact (a 2019 "I just wanted you to know that I haven't forgotten...") and no contentless closer, and his measured closers ("Let me know if you need anything." "Happy to hop on a call.") trip neither rule. A motive phrase followed by a "that" clause passes, because the clause carries the news. The motive rule is HARD because in that last position it carries no fact. The gate rule stays ADVISORY because Rock sometimes wants a real gate.

Fifth maintainer note: 1.7 changes only how this file tells you to run the linter. On 2026-10-31 Rock traced his repeat permission prompts to how sessions ran it. The old template named a relative `scripts/` path that does not exist from the working directory, so sessions expanded it on their own, added `-I`, and appended `; echo "exit=$?"` to read the exit code. Rock's allow rule is `Bash(python3 -I <full path to slop_lint.py> *)`, and it matches from the first character, so a chained line or a call without `-I` stops for a manual approval every time. The template below is now the exact call, and `coffee-peer-voice` 1.16 and `clean-user-facing-text` use the same shape. Keep it that way.

Sixth maintainer note: 1.8 came out of a 2026-11-04 LinkedIn comment on Gadi Evron's Adversary Brains post. The draft opened on "Taking ACH from seven hypotheses to thousands is the headline, and it deserves to be," and followed it with "the math is the best part." Rock flagged both as sycophantic. The Voice Corrections Register already held the same tell from the 2026-10-26 Helen Oakley comment ("the feature I'd show every AppSec team first," "I'm stealing," "go kick the tires"), and slop_lint 1.4.0 returned HARD 0 on both drafts. Two register hits meet the register's own threshold for a linter rule, so slop_lint 1.5.0 adds H-PRAISE-LABEL, HARD, for phrases that grade someone's work in place of saying what it does: "is the headline," "is the best part," a clause ending "and it deserves to be," "deserves more attention / eyes / credit," "the smartest thing in," "the feature I'd show every," "I'm stealing this," "kick the tires," "must-read," "unparalleled," and "couldn't be prouder." The rule is HARD because none of these carries a fact. Literal uses stay legal and the test file pins them: "the user deserves to be notified," "patching is the best thing you can do," "the headline number was 3,000." When it fires, delete the label and let the specific thing the work does carry the support.

Seventh maintainer note: 1.9 came out of a 2026-11-06 audit Rock asked for after too many register tells reached him. Every one of the register's 112 entries was read and mapped (`references/register-coverage.md`). 64 entries carry a span a regex can see, and only eight of them had a rule. slop_lint 1.6.0 adds the other 56 as rules, with each rule's comment naming its entry. The remaining 48 entries are stance, heat, audience, and fact calls that a regex would get wrong on Rock's own sentences, so they stay with the read-back pass. The same release adds shape rules from SlopShape (Madler, arXiv:2609.15369v3) and a burstiness check calibrated on Rock's samples. The second cause the audit found was sessions reading an ADVISORY finding and keeping the span without saying so, which is why the disposition rule below now makes every ADVISORY finding print a line. Do not remove that rule, because it is the only thing that makes an override visible.

Eighth maintainer note: 1.10 records Rock's November 6, 2026 Pangram test under "Shape tells" and adds his rule that this skill never calls a paid detector. He set the rule because each call bills his account. Do not remove it.

Ninth maintainer note: 1.11 came out of a 2026-11-10 LinkedIn Comment Brief option, "Agreed on the hygiene, and your runtime question is the one I'd put first," which slop_lint 1.6.0 passed with HARD 0 and ADVISORY 0. The register's 2026-11-04 x reply entry had turned one rewrite of Rock's into the rule "open the reply in agreement," and the brief's prompt copied it, so 10 of 25 reply drafts from November 8 to 10 opened "Agreed." The register entry and the prompt are fixed. slop_lint 1.7.0 adds three HARD rules so the template can't come back through a new instruction: H-AGREE-RAMP for "Agreed" used as a hinge into a clause, H-AGREE-REPEAT for a second agreement opener of any kind in one social batch, and H-RANK-PICK for a ranking verdict on someone's point with any verb ("is the one I'd put first," "the part I'd keep"). H-POINTBACK listed only negative verbs, which is how "put first" got through. The new rules match the shape, so the next synonym needs no register entry. They fire 0 times on Rock's voice samples. H-AGREE-RAMP fires on one of his 50 published X replies from November 3 to 10, the 2026-11-04 line that seeded the template, and Rock approved it as HARD on 2026-11-10 because the linter only reads drafts.

The rest went to about ten model round trips, four of them spent undoing a sentence-rhythm regression the rewrite introduced and one on a fact check nobody asked for. 1.12 adds the step budget under "Run the linter first," adds `scripts/slop_pass.py` so the lint, the Unicode check, and the stylometry gauge run in one call, and moves the first nine notes out of SKILL.md into this file so they stop loading on every call.
