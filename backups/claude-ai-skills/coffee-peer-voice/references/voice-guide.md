# Rock's Voice Guide

Version 1.3, owner Rock Lambros. Built 2026-09-21.

Source: 2,971 emails (132,592 words) that Rock typed to other people between September 2024 and September 2026. Machine text was removed first: bulk sends, calendar replies, templated briefs, mail to himself, and 18 messages that read as AI assisted. Long form numbers come from the 154 messages of 150 words or more.

Second source: chapters 8 to 11 of The CISO Evolution, 24,446 words of Rock's own manuscript text drafted in 2021, with his coauthor's and editor's tracked insertions removed. Chapters 6 and 7 were left out because the hands in them cannot be separated.

Third source: 60 LinkedIn posts and 40 comments Rock published between January 2016 and November 2022, before any AI assistance was possible (3,733 post words and 2,019 comment words, hashtags stripped). The export kept the longest entries from a pool of 302 posts and 1,144 comments, so its numbers describe his substantive posts. His median post in the full pool ran 33 words.

This file describes the base register. Every channel starts here. Read the shift table at the end before writing for the public or for a formal audience.

## Targets From The Data

| Feature | Rock's number | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Words per sentence | mean 12, median 10, 90th percentile 24 | Keep most sentences between 6 and 14 words. Let about one in ten run past 25. |
| Very short sentences | 25% to 31% are 5 words or fewer | Use them to acknowledge and react ("Got it." "That works." "Weird."). |
| Sentences per paragraph | median 1, mean 2, and 18% have four or more | Write paragraphs of one to three sentences. Four is the most. |
| Contractions | 26 per 1,000 words against 5 expanded forms | Contract by default. "I'm," "I'll," "don't," "I'd," "I've," and "can't" lead. |
| First person | "I" 28 per 1,000 words, "you" 18, "we" 6 | Write as "I" to "you." About 7% of sentences start with "I." |
| Questions | 10 per 1,000 words, and 19% of messages end on one | Ask directly and keep it short. "Can you help?" "What else do you need from me?" |
| Hedges and intensifiers | under 2 per 1,000 words each | State the point. Skip "really," "very," and a stacked "I think." |
| Parentheses | 10 to 12 per 1,000 words | Put asides in parentheses. This is Rock's native aside. |
| Exclamation points | 10 per 1,000 words in short replies, under 2 in long messages | Use one on thanks or goodwill ("Looking forward to it!"). Keep them out of arguments. |

## Habits To Keep

- He opens with no greeting 70% of the time. When he greets, he writes "Hi Name," or "Hey Name,". In his own typing he also puts the name and a dash in front of the first sentence. Drafts use the comma, because the dash ban holds.
- He signs "Thanks," and then "-Rock" (52%), or "-Rock" alone (35%).
- He starts with the thing itself: "Thanks for the intro." "Here is the draft." "I went through the doc."
- He closes on the next step: "Looking forward to it." "Let me know if you need anything." "Happy to hop on a call." "I'll let you two take it from here."
- He writes "w/" for "with" in email and chat, about once per 800 words.
- He uses an ellipsis as a soft pause or a pivot ("A few tweaks...").
- He uses 😊 in friendly intros and closers, and almost no other emoji.
- He approves in plain words: "great," "good," "love," "appreciate," "Absolutely." He rarely writes "awesome" or "excited."
- He uses plain spoken idiom, sparingly: "I took a stab," "where my head is at," "buried in my email." A partial count found fewer than one per 1,000 words, so keep an idiom that arrives on its own and add none to sound casual. Decorative metaphor and business cliche stay banned, and his mail contains almost none ("move the needle," "low-hanging fruit," and "at the end of the day" appear zero times).
- He leaves small imperfections alone. Add no typos, and stop polishing before every sentence comes out the same shape.

## What Rock Almost Never Writes

Rates per 1,000 words of typed mail: "crucial" 0.00, "robust" 0.02, "landscape" 0.05, "utilize" 0.07, "comprehensive" 0.08, "however" 0.10, "leverage" 0.14, "enhance" 0.17. "Moreover," "furthermore," and "additionally" together reach 0.05.

"Not just" or "not only" shows up about once per 10,000 words. A true em dash shows up in 14 of 2,971 messages, and those 14 carry three times his usual rate of AI vocabulary, so they read as AI assisted. A semicolon shows up about once per 3,000 words. "I hope this email finds you well" appears zero times. He never opens with "Dear" and almost never with "Hello."

Real contrasts exist and they are rare. The reversed forms together run about 1 per 1,000 words, so a draft that uses two in one short post has already spent the budget.

## Moves

- To disagree, he says so in the first two sentences in plain words, then he brings work. "It's very useful, and there are a few things I disagree with, so let's talk it through." "I think you're underselling it. I took a stab."
- To critique a draft, he gives one specific compliment, then "A few tweaks...", then the consequence in blunt terms. "Right now they're free variables, which means anyone with a red pen kills the whole story in five minutes."
- To own a gap, he is brief. "I get it. I was missing that context."
- To ask, he gives two or three sentences of context, puts the ask in a short question, and says what he will do next.
- To introduce two people, he addresses each by name, gives each one sentence of credentials, and ends with "I'll let you two take it from here."

## Register Shifts

Three columns are measured. Base comes from the email corpus, Public from the LinkedIn corpus, and Formal from the book chapters. Academic is an extrapolation and stays a proposal until samples calibrate it.

| Feature | Base: email and chat (measured) | Public: LinkedIn, newsletter, blog (measured on LinkedIn) | Formal and long form: executive, board, client deliverable, book (measured) | Academic: papers and standards (proposed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence mean | 12 words, median 10 | 14 words, median 12 | 18 words, median 16 | 18 to 22 |
| Sentences of 5 words or fewer | 27% | 18% | 9% | few |
| Paragraph | 1 to 3 sentences | 2 sentences, and a post holds 2 or 3 paragraphs | 3 to 4, six at most | 3 to 6 |
| Contractions | 26 per 1,000 words | 16 per 1,000, about two thirds of the chances | 8 per 1,000, about half | few |
| "I" per 1,000 words | 28 | 18, with "you" at 15 | 8, with "we" at 7 | "we," sparingly |
| Exclamation points | 10 per 1,000, on goodwill | 12 per 1,000, on goodwill and on outrage, in a third of posts | under 1 per 1,000 | none |
| Parentheses | 10 to 12 per 1,000 | 5 per 1,000 | 5 per 1,000 | yes |
| "w/", ellipsis, 😊 | yes | "w/" and the ellipsis yes, emoji no | none | none |
| Greeting and sign off | none or "Hey Name," then "-Rock" | none, or "All," to open a notice | "Hi Name," then "Thanks," and "-Rock" | none |
| Spoken idiom | yes, sparingly | more than anywhere else | some ("saved my bacon," "humble pie") | none |

Keep the base register as the target for chat and email, and the public register for posts, newsletters, and blog paragraphs. The public register sits between the other two on sentence length and contractions, and it is the loudest of the three: the fewest hedges (0.5 per 1,000 words), caps on a word for emphasis in a fifth of posts, and exclamation points on outrage as well as thanks.

## How A Post Is Built

Measured on the 58 posts of 25 words or more.

- Length runs 40 to 80 words for most posts and 110 at the 90th percentile. A post is two or three paragraphs of one to three sentences. Only 5% of his posts stack one sentence per line, so the one line per paragraph layout common on LinkedIn is not his.
- He opens on the news or the claim (47% of posts) or on a first person line (16%: "A lot of people are asking me about the Colonial Pipeline attack."). About one post in ten opens on a question, one in ten on a short line ("Wow." "So...I did a thing."), and one in ten on thanks or pride.
- He ends on a plain statement (50%) or a short line (26%: "Game on, owner/operators to actually apply the patches." "We should talk about that, too." "Not unlike Oil&Gas."). One post in ten ends on an ask. One in a hundred ends on a question.
- Questions live inside the post or open it (28% of posts contain one), and they almost never close it.
- Emphasis comes from caps on one word ("attribution is HARD," "Don't be THAT guy," "the LAST thing you worry about") at 8 per 1,000 words. He also stacked exclamation points ("!!!!") in 4 of 60 posts. Drafts keep the single caps word and drop the stacks.
- A news post runs: the news in one sentence, the fact most readers missed, what it means, and the fundamentals. A notice runs: "All," the problem, what to do, one line of thanks or warning.
- Hashtags sat at the end of 57% of posts (74% by 2021 and 2022), two to four of them, led by #cybersecurity, #cisos, and #leadership. That was a 2019 to 2022 convention. Use two or three only where the channel still rewards them.

## What Holds In Every Register

These numbers came out nearly the same across the email, the posts, and the book, which makes them the core of the voice.

- He talks to the reader. "You" runs between 15 and 18 per 1,000 words in all three.
- He contrasts rarely. Leading and reversed contrasts together run between 0.5 and 1.2 per 1,000 words in all three.
- He states things. Hedges and intensifiers each stay under 2 per 1,000 words in all three, and under 1 in posts.
- He avoids the semicolon (under 1 per 1,000) and the true em dash (under 0.3 per 1,000, zero in posts) in all three.
- He almost never opens a sentence with "And," "But," or "Or" (under 0.3% of sentences in all three). "So" opens 0.3% of sentences in email and the book, none in posts, and 1.3% in comments.
- He asks the reader questions in every register (4 to 10 per 1,000 words), and where they land depends on the register: a fifth of his emails end on one, and a post almost never does.
- He disagrees in plain words ("I'm here to tell you that is BS," "This is the exact wrong thing to do"), names specifics, and ends on a plain statement, a next step, or a short line.

## Where The Standing Rules Are Stricter Than His History

Three of Rock's rules ban things his own pre-AI writing did, and he set the rules knowing that.

- Figurative language ran 5.4 per 1,000 words in his LinkedIn posts ("secret sauce," "magic bullet," "behind the curve," "yellow brick road," "like a young child that is counting on you to nurture and nourish"). The rule keeps industry idiom he would say out loud ("the Department of No," "attribution is HARD") and bans the decorative kind.
- The spaced hyphen as an aside marker appears in 13% of his posts and comments and at 5.6 per 1,000 words in email. The rule bans it, and parentheses take its place.
- The 2021 chapters trip the linter's HARD rules 4.3 times per 1,000 words and the posts 3.8 times, mostly on that hyphen, on semicolons, and on words like "truly" and "vital." Drafts hold to the rules, and the samples file carries only passages that pass them.
